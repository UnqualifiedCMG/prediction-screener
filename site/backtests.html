Do the crowd's extremes resolve?

A prediction market priced at 90¢ is the crowd saying "this is 90% likely." Is the crowd right 90% of the time? We pulled every resolved Polymarket market we could get price history for — 1,490 markets, minimum $1,000 volume, daily price samples — and checked. Note: this backtest covers all market categories (sports, politics, crypto), not just sports. A sports-only backtest is on the roadmap.

1. Markets that touch 90¢ resolve YES 92% of the time

92.3% (n=736)

Of 736 markets that traded at or above 90¢ at any point, 679 resolved YES. The mirror image holds too: markets that touched 10¢ resolved NO 91.2% of the time (n=889). The full ladder:

Calibration looks roughly right: higher prices → higher hit rates, symmetric on both sides. The crowd isn't systematically overconfident at the extremes.

Takeaway: extreme prices are usually right — but "usually right at 90¢" is only useful if you can buy below 90¢. Which brings us to the catch.

2. The signal isn't touching 90¢ — it's holding it

Split the 736 markets that touched 90¢ by where they sat in the final week:

GroupNResolved YES
Touched 90¢, still ≥80¢ in final week677100.0%
Touched 90¢, collapsed below 80¢593.4%

Every single market that reached 90¢ and held above 80¢ into the final week resolved YES — 677 for 677. And of the 59 that touched 90¢ but collapsed back below 80¢, only 2 resolved YES. The collapse is the signal: when a 90¢ market starts sliding, the crowd is telling you something changed.

Takeaway: don't buy at 90¢ — watch for markets that touched 90¢ and are slipping. A breakdown below 80¢ after touching 90¢ resolved NO 96.6% of the time. That's the tradeable shape, not the headline number.

3. The hindsight problem

Here's why the 92.3% isn't free money. The median time from a market's first touch of 90¢ to resolution was 2.4 hours (n=736; mean 0.2 days). For the 10¢ touch: median 2.4 hours as well (n=890).

2.4 hours before resolution, the outcome is usually already decided — the goal has been scored, the vote counted, the announcement made. The market isn't predicting at 90¢; it's reporting. The 92.3% is mostly hindsight, not edge.

Takeaway: by the time a market prints 90¢, the information is public and the edge is gone. The real game is getting in before the crowd moves it there — which is what the Sports tab's divergence leaderboard tries to catch from the other direction.

Honest caveats

Daily price samples miss intraday spikes — a market that briefly touched 90¢ between samples counts as never reaching it. Only markets the API still lists are analyzed; deleted or voided markets are excluded, which can inflate hit rates (selection bias). These are unconditional averages across all categories — sports, politics, crypto — and base rates differ wildly by category. Buckets with small N are noise, not signal. Polymarket only: Kalshi has no price-history API, so its markets can't be analyzed this way.
